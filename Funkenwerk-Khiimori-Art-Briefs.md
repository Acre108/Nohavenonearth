# Funkenwerk and Khiimori Art Briefs

Concept art briefs for the six frames of two linked lines in the No Haven on Earth Atlas:

- **Funkenwerk Berlin:** the FK-2 Läufer (Gen 2), FK-5 Querschläger (Gen 3) and FK-X Aufbruch (Gen 4). Funkenwerk is a workers' cooperative in a squatted Tempelhof hangar. It was founded by the engineers and crews of the VK-1B Écuyer Blitz.
- **Khiimori Collective:** the KH-2 Takhi (Gen 2), KH-5 Bürged (Gen 3) and KH-X Tsagaan Salkhi (Gen 4). Khiimori is a cooperative of Mongolian rail workshops in Darkhan, building fast frames for the Pact's Steppe outriders.

The two lines meet in 2185. Funkenwerk splits over a Berlin state contract, and the members who walk out take the Querschläger drawings to Darkhan. The Tsagaan Salkhi is what the two shops build together.

## How to use this file (instructions for ChatGPT)

- Generate one image per request. Use the frame's **Scene prompt** for an illustration or its **Turnaround prompt** for a design sheet.
- Each prompt is self-contained: it already includes the frame description, the maker's house style for that generation, the setting-wide style and an "Avoid" list. Use it as written.
- Keep every image in the same style as the existing sets: grounded real-robot mecha concept art, landscape format (16:9 for scenes), no text or logos baked into the image. Graffiti, stickers and ornament are shapes and patterns, never readable words.
- The briefs are the source of truth. If a prompt and a brief disagree, follow the brief.
- **Reference images:**
  - Funkenwerk frames: attach the VK-1B Écuyer Blitz (`output/imagegen/variants/vk-1b-ecuyer-blitz-*`), their ancestor.
  - Khiimori Gen 2 and Gen 3: attach the Type-01M Steppe Tieniu (`output/imagegen/variants/13-type-01m-steppe-tieniu-*`), the stripped frame the outriders used before the Takhi.
  - The Tsagaan Salkhi: attach the finished Querschläger and Bürged images, since it is the meeting of the two.
- **Scale:** a person is about 1.8 m, roughly one tenth of a frame's height. On turnaround sheets the human figure should reach only to the frame's ankle or lower shin.
- None of these frames has an energy effect. Sensors may glow faintly; weapons and engines do not. Rocket exhaust and muzzle flash are fire, not energy, and are allowed.

## Design language: from haphazard to sleek

Each line starts rough and ends sleek. The two Gen 4 frames show what each house became.

| | Gen 2 | Gen 3 | Gen 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Funkenwerk** | Punk: exposed tube, bolt-on panels, crew-painted in clashing colours, sticker-bombed | Patchwork: panels from six workshops in six colours, antennas, a pirate-radio rig | Military: sealed faceted hull, matt black, uniform. One hidden crew mark is all that is left of the punks. |
| **Khiimori** | Workshop: welded tube space-frame like a rally truck, ugly welds, felt cab, strapped-on bundles | Raider: hand-bent tube, salvaged green Tieshan parts, a hunched eagle posture | Convergence: Darkhan's long legs, now machined, under Berlin's sealed panels, painted matt white |

**Shared family traits** that carry through every frame and meet in the Tsagaan Salkhi:

- **Funkenwerk** keeps the Écuyer's descendant head: a narrow visor with a single mono-eye behind it. It gets narrower and flatter each generation.
- **Khiimori** keeps long, lightly built legs and a forward-leaning gait. From the Bürged on, the head sits under a hooded cowl like a falconer's eagle hood.
- **Both** are runners: light bodies, long legs, the weight carried high and forward.
- **Marks:**
  - Funkenwerk crews paint a small hand-drawn spark: a zigzag spark shape, with no letters.
  - Khiimori crews tie blue silk khadag scarves to the frame and paint the endless-knot (ulzii) ornament on panels.

## Art direction for the whole setting

- Real robot, not super robot. These frames are 16–19 m machines that look buildable and serviceable.
- Show the power plant. Fuel-cell modules, reformer stacks, multi-fuel diesels and exhaust stacks are part of each silhouette.
- Always give scale: trucks, people, horses, ground crew, buildings or trees.
- Paint, stencils and wear tell the story.

**Setting style, Gen 2 and Gen 3 (included in those prompts):** grounded real-robot mecha concept art, believable military hardware, visible joints, hydraulic pistons and armour panel lines, stencilled markings, realistic weathering with chipped paint and grime, clear scale reference with vehicles or people nearby, early-generation technology with no glowing energy effects, detailed industrial design, cinematic lighting.

**Setting style, Gen 4 (included in those prompts):** grounded real-robot mecha concept art, believable military hardware, visible joints, hydraulic pistons and armour panel lines, stencilled markings, realistic weathering, clear scale reference with vehicles or people nearby, detailed industrial design, cinematic lighting.

**Avoid (included in every prompt):** super robot proportions, glowing energy wings, glowing weapons, magic effects, chrome, organic or biomechanical forms, fantasy armour, cape, oversized head, chibi, cute, extra limbs, deformed hands, blurry, text, watermark, logo, readable lettering, copies of existing anime designs.

## Funkenwerk Berlin

*Continental Union*

A workers' cooperative in a squatted hangar at Tempelhof, founded in 2172 by the Kassel engineers and Blitz crews who walked out when Vauclair-Ehrlich scrapped the Blitz. Every member has one vote and every drawing is published. Its first two frames look like they were built by a punk collective, because they were. Its third was built to a state contract, and it shows.

**House style, Gen 2 and Gen 3:** Funkenwerk design language: a light lean runner frame descended from the Écuyer, exposed tube structure with bolt-on panels left raw or crew-painted in clashing colours, deliberately asymmetric armour, hand-cut panel edges, stencil-art and sticker-bombed surfaces with no readable lettering, cylindrical VEK-style fuel-cell modules exposed on the back, a narrow visor head with a single mono-eye behind it, a small hand-painted zigzag spark mark.

**House style, Gen 4:** late Funkenwerk design language: a lean runner frame grown up, sealed faceted armour in uniform matt black with crisp panel lines, a narrow flat visor with a single mono-eye behind it, integrated fuel-cell and capacitor housings, disciplined military finish, a single small hand-painted zigzag spark hidden under the shoulder as the only trace of its punk origins.

### FK-2 Läufer

Gen 2 · 17.4 m · 46 t · The Blitz's heir, built in a squatted hangar

- **Silhouette:** A light runner: lighter than an Écuyer, long-legged and leaning forward. The Blitz's stripped look turned into a design choice rather than a field refit.
- **Head:** The Écuyer family head made narrower and lower: a slim visor slit with a single mono-eye on a track behind it. The sensor mast is gone for good.
- **Torso:** A slim chest of exposed welded tube, with bolt-on panels in different colours and finishes, some raw metal, some hand-painted. Sticker-bombed shoulder panels.

- **Legs:** Long running legs with exposed thrust rods and ankle dampers, the Blitz's re-geared stride made standard. Mud-flap guards.
- **Back & power:** A BZ-4 modular cell stack: three or four cylindrical fuel-cell modules plugged into a rack on the back like oversized batteries, each painted differently.
- **Weapons:** G35 telescoped rifle: a compact, boxy 35 mm rifle with a short barrel and a sensor sight.
- **Colours:** No two match. This one: a grey-blue base inherited from the Écuyer, one arm hot pink, one thigh panel raw primer, a black-and-yellow hazard-striped knee, stencil-art shapes and stickers, a small zigzag spark on the chest.
- **Details:** Hand-cut panel edges, visible bolt heads, cable ties, a crew's tool roll strapped to the hip.
- **Shot:** Night at Tempelhof. The hangar's huge doors are open on its assembly bays, lit by work lamps and string lights, and its walls are covered in graffiti. A Läufer steps out onto the old airfield apron while visitors, bicycles and a food stall crowd the edge of the bay to watch. Crew members in mismatched overalls are still painting its other arm.

### FK-5 Querschläger

Gen 3 · 17.8 m · 49 t · Open-drawing raider that hunts jammers, built by six collectives

- **Shot:** A rain-soaked night in a container port. Two Querschläger move between stacked containers, antennas up, their panels in visibly different colours. ### FK-X Aufbruch

Gen 4 · prototype, 2186 · 18.2 m · 52 t · The punks grown up: black, sealed and fast · Two flying

## Khiimori Collective

*Eurasian Pact · Beijing's sphere*

A cooperative of rail workshops in Darkhan, named for the wind horse on the prayer flags. From about 2175 it built fast frames for the Steppe outriders who scout the frame-train routes. Its engineering is rally trucks, tracklayers and herders' motorcycles: weld what you have, carry your own spares, never trust a depot. In 2185 the Funkenwerk splinter arrives, and the two styles meet.

**House style, Gen 2 and Gen 3:** Khiimori design language: a light fast runner built in rail workshops, a welded steel tube space-frame like a rally truck with thin bolted panels, long lightly built legs adapted from tracklayer suspension, a forward-leaning gait, a felt-lined cab with a canvas cover, fuel bladders and spare-part bundles strapped on like saddlebags, blue silk khadag scarves tied to the frame, faded sky-blue and dusty white paint with hand-painted endless-knot (ulzii) ornament on the panels.

**House style, Gen 4:** late Khiimori design language where Mongolian workshop and Berlin engineering meet: long machined runner legs from the steppe workshops under sealed faceted armour from Berlin, low and swept back, a hooded cowl over a narrow visor, integrated saddlebag fuel cells, matt black with blue khadag scarves still tied at the shoulders and a faint endless-knot ornament etched into the panels.

### KH-2 Takhi

Gen 2 · 16.2 m · 42 t · Wild horse: a welded-tube runner from the Darkhan sheds

- **Silhouette:** Light, rangy and a little crude: a welded tube frame on long legs, leaning forward like a running horse. Rougher than any Union frame, but clearly built to go fast.
- **Head:** A small boxy sensor head on a short neck, with two camera lenses and a fold-down sun visor. A tall radio whip.
- **Torso:** A welded steel tube space-frame like a rally truck's roll cage, with thin bolted panels that do not cover everything. A felt-lined cab in the chest with a canvas cover laced over it, big enough to sleep in. A small stove pipe pokes out of the cab roof.
- **Arms:** Thin arms with exposed hydraulics and truck-style grab handles.
- **Legs:** The defining feature: long legs built from tracklayer suspension parts, with coil springs and dampers in plain view, and wide splayed feet for grass and sand.
- **Back & power:** A tractor's multi-fuel diesel in a cage on the back, with one tall exhaust stack and a rain cap. Fuel bladders and bundles of spare parts strapped on like saddlebags.
- **Weapons:** Type-07 drum rifle: a reverse-engineered M30 with a large drum magazine. A carbon blade on the hip.
- **Colours:** Faded sky blue and dusty white, with hand-painted endless-knot ornament on the panels. Blue khadag scarves tied to the frame tubes and fluttering behind.
- **Details:** Ugly, visible welds. Dust everywhere. A sheepskin over the cab seat. A spare wheel-bearing bundle wired to a leg.
- **Shot:** Summer on the open steppe. A Takhi runs flat out beside a long freight frame train on the Trans-Mongolian line, keeping pace with it. Herders on horseback watch from a low ridge, with a white ger camp and grazing horses behind them under an enormous sky.
