# Gen 1 Frame Art Briefs

Concept art briefs for all 9 Generation 1 mecha frames in the No Haven on Earth Atlas. Gen 1 covers roughly 25 to 15 years before the present: the first machines that ever walked into combat.

## How to use this file (instructions for ChatGPT)

- Generate one image per request. Use the frame's **Scene prompt** for an illustration or its **Turnaround prompt** for a design sheet.
- Each prompt is self-contained: it already includes the frame description, the maker's early house style, the Gen 1 era style, the setting-wide style and an "Avoid" list. Use it as written.
- Keep every image in the same style as the Gen 2 set: grounded real-robot mecha concept art, landscape format (16:9 for scenes), no text or logos baked into the image.
- The briefs are the source of truth. If a prompt and a brief disagree, follow the brief.
- If a Gen 2 descendant image is attached for reference, match its maker's family resemblance, but make the Gen 1 frame older, cruder and heavier-looking. It must never look like a later or more advanced model.
- **Scale:** a person is about 1.8 m, roughly one tenth of a frame's height. On turnaround sheets the human figure should reach only to the frame's ankle or lower shin. Earlier images drew people far too large, which makes the frames read at half size.
- The AW-3 Lifter has four arms on purpose. The MR-1 Kizuna is unarmed on purpose.

## What makes Gen 1 different from Gen 2

Gen 1 frames are the ancestors. Every one of them is a first attempt, and it should show.

- **Improvised, not designed.** Each frame is built from something else: tank armour, construction-walker legs, excavator drive trains, truck diesels, submarine fuel cells, airliner batteries. Let the source machine show through.
- **Cruder construction.** Flat bolted and riveted plates instead of shaped armour. Exposed hydraulic rams, hoses and cable bundles. Big access hatches, grab rails and ladders. Fewer, thicker parts.
- **Weapons are bolted on.** Guns are vehicle weapons with a grip added; missiles are infantry tubes strapped to a shoulder; the first railgun is tethered to its backpack by a cable.
- **House styles are only starting.** You should be able to see where each maker's Gen 2 look comes from, but in rough form. Halcyon has no wing binders yet except on the Merlin. VEK's first frame is still a licensed Brannock design with a new head.
- **Older world.** Support vehicles, aircraft and uniforms are a generation older than in the Gen 2 art: boxier trucks, older tanks, older helicopters and tiltrotors.
- **Heavier wear.** These frames have been in service a long time. Faded paint, overpainted markings, repaired panels in a different shade, rust at the seams.

## Art direction for the whole setting

- Real robot, not super robot. Frames are 11–18 m military or industrial machines that look buildable, serviceable and mass-produced.
- Show the power plant. Turbine exhausts, battery spines, fuel-cell tanks, diesel stacks and capacitor backpacks are part of each silhouette.
- No glowing energy. Sensors may glow faintly; weapons do not. The only bright effect allowed is the Lifter's industrial cutting torch, drawn as a welding flame, not a beam.
- Always give scale: trucks, infantry, ground crew, rescue workers, buildings or trees.
- Paint, stencils and wear tell the story.
- Reference points: Votoms, Patlabor, Front Mission, Mobile Suit Gundam: The 08th MS Team, and the Zaku I / GM-era "first mass-produced machine" feel. Take the feel only; copy no existing designs.

**Gen 1 era style (included in every prompt):** first-generation mech from twenty years ago, cruder and heavier than later designs, bolted and riveted flat armour plates, exposed hydraulic rams, hoses and cable bundles, parts adapted from tanks, aircraft and construction machinery, faded and repainted military finish, period vehicles a generation old.

**Setting style (included in every prompt):** grounded real-robot mecha concept art, believable military hardware, visible joints, hydraulic pistons and armour panel lines, stencilled markings, realistic weathering with chipped paint and grime, clear scale reference with vehicles or people nearby, early-generation technology with no glowing energy effects, detailed industrial design, cinematic lighting.

**Avoid (included in every prompt):** super robot proportions, glowing energy wings, magic effects, chrome, organic or biomechanical forms, fantasy armour, cape, oversized head, chibi, cute, extra limbs, deformed hands, blurry, text, watermark, logo, sleek modern streamlined armour, stealth panelling. (The Lifter's prompt drops "extra limbs"; the Kizuna's adds "weapons, guns".)

## Halcyon Aeronautics

*United States · Air Force*

In Gen 1, Halcyon is an aircraft company learning to build mechs. Its first production frame is a licensed Brannock Garrison with the armour cut back; its first original design, the Merlin, is the first mech that can fold into a flight mode, slowly. Aircraft logic is already visible: access panels, intakes, aviation stencils and the single slit visor. Navy blue and white, with high-visibility orange.

**Early house style:** early Halcyon Aeronautics design language: aircraft engineering applied to a heavy mech frame, aircraft-style access panels and intakes, single horizontal slit visor, navy blue and white with small high-visibility orange accents, aviation stencils and squadron numbers.

### HX-4 Merlin

Gen 1 · 17.5 m · 39 t · First variable frame; transformed slowly. A dozen built.

- **Silhouette:** Taller and thinner than any other Gen 1 frame, but still heavy and awkward. Two short, stubby folded wings lie flat against the back, hinged on big exposed actuators. It looks like an aircraft that has been folded up, not a sleek fighter.
- **Head:** Boxy head with one horizontal slit visor and a pitot-tube-style antenna. A hinged nose fairing sits above it, ready to swing down in flight mode.
- **Torso:** Narrow chest with aircraft access panels, a small canopy-like viewing window and side intakes. Heavy hinge assemblies at the waist and hips, visibly built for the transformation.
- **Arms:** Thin arms with exposed hydraulic rams. Stowage clamps on the hips for the rifle, which must be put away before transforming.
- **Legs:** Long legs with electric ducted-fan nozzles at the back of the calves, driven by the battery spine, mounted on exposed gimbal frames. Cable runs and hydraulic lines along the outside of the shins.
- **Back & power:** A fixed BP-1 battery spine (sealed, with charging ports, no quick-swap handles) and a large, separate Mk.I capacitor backpack, boxy and armoured, sitting on top of it.
- **Weapons:** LR-1 linear rifle: the first mech railgun. Long, heavy, twin-rail barrel with a thick armoured power cable running from the stock to the capacitor backpack. Eight shots per charge.
- **Colours:** Navy blue and white with broad high-visibility orange test bands on the wings and limbs, as if it never fully left trials. Faded paint, an airframe number on the leg.
- **Details:** NO STEP stencils on the wings, scorched rails on the rifle, a hand-painted kill tally from the Caspian Crisis, wear around the hinges.
- **Shot:** The Battle of the Talysh Mountains. A Merlin kneels on a misty mountain ridge at dawn, firing the LR-1, its power cable trailing back to the backpack. Far below in the valley, a column of squat egg-shaped enemy mechs burns, well out of their own gun range.

### HX-2 Sparrowhawk

Gen 1 · 16.8 m · 44 t · Licensed Garrison, lightened for air drops

- **Silhouette:** Clearly an M4 Garrison underneath (same boxy hull and crouched legs) but visibly stripped. Armour plates are cut back, leaving the internal frame, hydraulics and cabling exposed at the shoulders, hips and thighs. Lighter and leaner than its parent.
- **Head:** The Garrison's low boxy head refitted with a Halcyon single slit visor and an aircraft-style blade antenna.
- **Torso:** Garrison hull with a thinner, lighter front plate. Four heavy lifting eyes on the shoulders and back for the tiltrotor sling, with reinforced frames around them.
- **Arms:** Garrison arms with the outer armour removed. Holds an M30.
- **Legs:** Garrison legs with the shin armour cut away, reinforced shock absorbers on the knees and ankles for hard landings.
- **Back & power:** A BP-1 battery spine in place of the Garrison's turbine: no exhaust, flat sealed battery housing with charging ports.
- **Weapons:** M30 hand cannon: a stubby 30 mm chain gun with a pistol grip, shoulder brace and 120-round box magazine.
- **Colours:** Navy blue and white Air Force paint sprayed over the Army's olive drab, which shows through at chips and edges. Orange markings around the lifting eyes.
- **Details:** Sling-point stencils, scuffed paint where the sling cables rub, landing-impact dents on the feet.
- **Shot:** Dusk over arid foothills. A Sparrowhawk hangs from cables under a heavy-lift tiltrotor, legs braced, moments before release. A second tiltrotor trails smoke in the distance.

## Brannock-Hale Heavy Industries

*United States · Army*

The Garrison is the root of almost every Western frame, so it should be the clearest "first mech" image in the set. A construction walker's legs under a tank's armour and fire control. Slab-sided, front-heavy, slow and loud, with one big turbine exhaust. Olive drab with white stencilled serials.

**Early house style:** early Brannock-Hale design language: tank armour on construction-walker legs, slab-sided boxy armour with a sloped glacis plate, crouched heavy legs, one large turbine exhaust on the back, grab handles, tow hooks and whip antennas, olive drab military paint with white stencilled serial numbers.

### M4 Garrison

Gen 1 · 17.2 m · 61 t · The first mech in combat service

- **Silhouette:** Wide, slab-sided and front-heavy, with a crouched stance. Simpler and more box-like than the Teamster: flat plates, few angles, very little shaping. It should read as a tank hull standing up.
- **Head:** A tank commander's cupola with vision blocks around the sides, a single fire-control sight box and a whip antenna.
- **Torso:** A tank's sloped glacis on the front, with a top hatch for the cockpit. Riveted side plates, tow hooks at the waist, a jerrycan rack.
- **Arms:** Thick, simple arms with big exposed hydraulic rams. The left forearm carries a PB-1 pile bunker: a hydraulic steel stake in a heavy sleeve.
- **Legs:** Construction-walker legs: industrial, with large exposed hydraulic cylinders, broad flat feet and leg guards bolted on afterwards.
- **Back & power:** A GT-900 tank turbine in the rear hull, with one large rectangular louvred exhaust that throws heat shimmer. Fuel tanks strapped either side.
- **Weapons:** M30 hand cannon in the right hand (stubby 30 mm chain gun, pistol grip, shoulder brace, 120-round box). M2 kit: two infantry ATGM tubes strapped to the shoulders.
- **Colours:** Olive drab, faded and patched. White serial numbers, a unit number on the shoulder, yellow tie-down markings.
- **Details:** Spare road wheels or track links hung as extra armour, mud to the knees, exhaust soot across the back.
- **Shot:** Operation Iron Gate, in dusty foothills. Four Garrisons rise out of a steep ravine. The lead one fires an ATGM; a tank column burns on the road above. Turbine heat haze, dust everywhere.

## Vauclair-Ehrlich Konsortium

*Continental Union*

VEK's first frame is a licence-built Garrison, so it is boxy underneath. What makes it VEK is the new head, the first mono-eye in the setting, and a near-silent submarine fuel cell in place of the turbine. A few rounded panels hint at the knightly armour of the Haubert to come. Grey-blue and white.

**Early house style:** early Vauclair-Ehrlich design language: a licensed boxy tank-like frame softened with a few rounded VEK armour panels, the first mono-eye sensor on a track behind a narrow visor slit, cylindrical fuel-cell tanks on the back, grey-blue and white paint, small national flag flash on the shoulder.

### VK-1 Écuyer

Gen 1 · 17.2 m · 59 t · Licensed Garrison with VEK sensors

- **Silhouette:** Garrison proportions (boxy, crouched, front-heavy) but standing a little taller and cleaner. Rounded shoulder caps and a rounded head break up the box shape. No exhaust plume.
- **Head:** A new rounded helmet-like sensor head with a narrow horizontal visor slit and a single mono-eye on a track behind it, glowing faintly. A tall telescoping sensor mast on one side.
- **Torso:** The Garrison's glacis and hull, with a few rounded add-on panels and extra sensor housings at the collar.
- **Arms:** Garrison arms with rounded shoulder caps. PB-1 pile bunker on the left forearm.
- **Legs:** Garrison legs, unchanged, with bolted-on rounded knee guards.
- **Back & power:** A BZ-1 submarine-derived fuel cell: two or three large cylindrical hydrogen pressure tanks mounted horizontally on the back, with valves, pipework and warning stripes. No turbine exhaust.
- **Weapons:** M30 hand cannon in the right hand.
- **Colours:** Grey-blue and white, a small member-state flag flash on the shoulder. The Garrison's olive drab shows at worn edges.
- **Details:** Hydrogen hazard markings on the tanks, a fuelling port on the hip, frost on the tank valves.
- **Shot:** Directive 1, the mech cavalry screen. An Écuyer stands at the edge of a European forest at dawn, sensor mast raised, scanning across misty farmland. A column of older main battle tanks waits behind it on a farm track.

## Boreal Dynamics

*Canada · a US ally*

Canada's first frames are Brannock M4 Garrisons built under licence in Calgary, then winterised by people who have spent decades keeping mining haulers running across ice roads. Underneath it is still a Garrison. What makes it Boreal is the padding: quilted thermal covers bolted over every joint, wide snowshoe plates, heater hoses everywhere. Winter white over olive drab, with safety-orange bands.

**Early house style:** early Boreal Dynamics design language: a licensed boxy tank-like frame winterised for the Arctic, quilted thermal covers bolted over the joints, wide snowshoe plates on the feet, heater hoses and an engine pre-heater on the back, winter white sprayed over olive drab with safety-orange bands.

### BD-2 Tundra

**Revised 19 Oct 2026 · Regenerate scene and turnaround.** The first images gave it a rotary gun, drew the scale figure about twice too tall, and set the scene at a clear sunset on a mountain road. Keep from those images: the quilted covers, the bolted snowshoe plates with ice cleats, the louvred pre-heater on the chest, the red cold-start drum, the orange arm bands, the roof spotlight and the white-over-olive finish.
