## How to use this file (instructions for ChatGPT)

- Generate one image per request. Use the frame's **Scene prompt** for an illustration or its **Turnaround prompt** for a design sheet.
- Each prompt is self-contained and already includes the style and "Avoid" list it needs. Use it as written.
- The briefs are the source of truth. If a prompt and a brief disagree, follow the brief.
- **Scale:** a person is about 1.8 m, roughly one tenth of a frame's height. On turnaround sheets the human figure should reach only to the frame's ankle or lower shin.
- The **Tieshan-I** is drawn like every other frame in the Gen 1–3 sets: grounded real-robot concept art, landscape 16:9 scenes, no text or logos.
- The **two Unacknowledged frames are deliberately different.** Nobody in the setting has a clean look at them, and the art must not give the viewer one either. Read the next section before generating them.

## The Unacknowledged treatment

These frames are known only from evidence: a soldier's camera, a patrol's long lens, an intelligence analyst's reconstruction. The art should feel like a file that someone was not meant to see, not a design reveal.

**Scenes are evidence photographs, not illustrations.**
- Shot by someone who was not ready: a helmet camera, a handheld phone, a telephoto lens from a moving boat.
- Badly framed: the camera is tilted (a Dutch angle of 10–25 degrees), too low or too high, and the frame is partly cut off by the image edge.
- Badly focused: motion blur on the frame, or focus caught on something in the foreground (a fence, rigging, rain on the lens) while the frame stays soft.
- Partly hidden: smoke, haze, heat shimmer, spray, darkness or foreground objects cover at least a third of it.
- Bad exposure: blown-out highlights from fire or sun, crushed shadows, heavy sensor grain or film grain, a slight colour cast.
- The frame is still recognisable as a real robot: you can pick out one or two telling details (named in each brief), and nothing else is certain.
- No legible text anywhere. A camera data strip may appear as blank or unreadable marks only.

**Technical sheets are redacted intelligence reconstructions, not clean turnarounds.**
- Drawn from photographs by an analyst, so lines are confident where the photos were clear and **dashed or ghosted where they were not**.
- **Solid black rectangular redaction bars** cover specific areas named in each brief (the cockpit, the power plant, the fibre callouts), plus the measurements.
- Fewer views than a normal sheet: at least one view panel is missing, left as an empty box or blacked out entirely.
- Callout leader lines run to boxes that are blacked out.
- Paper texture: photocopied, slightly skewed on the page, a punched-hole margin, a torn or folded corner, an archive stamp shape with no readable lettering.
- Keep the human scale figure and an unlabelled scale bar.

**What the fibre should look like.** Both frames have "synthetic muscle": bundles of wound grey-red fibre that run along the limbs between armour plates, like cable looms that tighten and flex. It sits on a visible metal skeleton with hydraulic parts still in place. It is **not** flesh, skin, bone, tentacles or an alien creature. The frame is a crude real robot with something wrong about its joints.

**Avoid (Unacknowledged frames):** clean studio lighting, sharp focus on the mech, heroic pose, centred composition, full clear view of the mech, super robot proportions, glowing energy, magic effects, chrome, alien creature, horror monster, H.R. Giger style, exposed bones, skin, gore, blood, tentacles, extra limbs, fantasy armour, oversized head, legible text, readable numbers, watermark, logo, copies of existing anime designs.

## Tianlong State Machinery, in Indian service

*Bought from the rival, maintained on its schedule*

**House style:** Tianlong design language: squat mass-produced mech with a rounded egg-shaped armoured torso, short thick limbs, small domed head with a lens cluster, cast armour texture with visible weld seams, twin diesel exhaust stacks on the back. In Indian service it is repainted, and the Chinese paint shows through wherever the Indian crews didn't reach.

### Type-09I Tieshan-I

Gen 2 · 16.8 m · 70 t · Export Tieshans bought by Delhi in 2173 after a decade of raids it couldn't answer. Indian depots added bigger radiators and a second radio; every spare part comes through a Tianlong export depot, released when Tianshu schedules it.

- **Silhouette:** A Type-09 Tieshan, squat and egg-shaped on short thick limbs, with two Indian additions that break its outline: an oversized radiator box bolted to the back between the exhaust stacks, and a second, taller radio mast on the left shoulder.
- **Head:** Tieshan dome with the three-lens cluster, plus a locally made sun hood of riveted sheet steel.
- **Torso:** Cast Tieshan hull with weld seams. A bolted-on Indian equipment box on the right side for the second radio.
- **Arms:** Short Tieshan arms with three-fingered hands.
- **Legs:** Stubby Tieshan legs with round knee caps, worn from kneeling.
- **Back & power:** Type-06 multi-fuel diesel with twin sooty stacks, and the large added radiator box with a louvred grille and a fan shroud.
- **Weapons:** Type-09 200 mm long cannon with folding bipod; Type-07 drum rifle as a sidearm.
- **Colours:** Indian Army dusty olive and sand disruptive camouflage, brushed on over factory dark green. The original dark green and rust-red trim show at the joints, inside panel edges and on the parts the crews never repainted. Indian tactical numbers stencilled in white over ground-off Chinese ones.
- **Details:** A sealed parts cage or crate with a heavy export seal on it. Empty actuator bays, missing panels, frames on jacks. These machines spend a lot of time waiting.
- **Shot:** Dawn at a depot outside Ahmedabad, the morning after the Kandla raid. Black smoke rises on the horizon. Three Tieshan-Is stand in the yard: one is on jacks with its knee actuator missing, one has a crew leaning against its foot, and one is moving out alone. In front of them, a chain-link parts cage holds crates under a heavy foreign seal, and an Indian officer stands at the locked gate.

## Unacknowledged frames

*Seen, photographed or recovered, and claimed by no maker*

**House style (both frames):** crude kitbashed real-robot frame in the desert-workshop tradition, parts from rival blocs welded together, raw primer and flaking paint with no unit markings, gaps between armour plates filled with bundles of wound grey-red synthetic fibre that run along the limbs like cable looms and flex like muscle over a visible metal skeleton and hydraulic pistons.

### IA-R3 Rakshasa

Gen 3 · est. 16 m · Indian Army designation for the raid frames that came ashore at Ratnagiri in 2180 and burned the fuel depot in forty minutes. The soldiers called them rakshasa, demons.

- **What the evidence shows:** Brannock-Hale Garrison hips and legs (industrial construction-walker legs, big exposed hydraulic cylinders, broad flat feet) under a Tieniu-style egg-shaped cast torso, welded together the way the desert workshops build Haboobs. The arms and thighs have armour plates missing on purpose, and the gaps are packed with bundled grey-red fibre that shines wetly in firelight. Its stance is wrong for a machine of its weight: low, fast, leaning into the stride.
- **Head:** A small dome with a mismatched lens cluster, half of it covered by a welded plate.
- **Weapons:** A drum-fed rifle held in one hand, carried like a club.
- **Colours:** Rust, raw primer and flaking sand paint, with no markings at all.
- **The two details the photo must catch:** the bundled fibre in a limb gap, and the lunging stride. Everything else can be lost in smoke and blur.
- **Scene (evidence photo):** Night at the Ratnagiri fuel depot, taken on an Indian Army soldier's helmet camera from behind sandbags. The fuel tanks are burning. The Rakshasa crosses the frame mid-stride through the firelight and is motion-blurred. The camera is tilted about 20 degrees, the out-of-focus top of a sandbag and a chain-link fence fill the bottom third, and the fire blows out the highlights. The frame's upper body is cut off by the top edge of the image. Heavy grain and a sickly orange colour cast.
- **Technical sheet (redacted reconstruction):** An Indian Army intelligence reconstruction drawn from photographs. Only front and side views are drawn. The back view is dashed outline only, and the three-quarter panel is an empty box with a black bar across it. An inset shows a cross-section of the recovered severed forearm, with the fibre bundles drawn around a metal bone-like strut, and its callout box is blacked out. **Redaction bars** cover the cockpit, the power plant, the fibre callouts and all measurements. Photocopied paper with a punched-hole margin and a folded corner.

### QX-██ The Hafun frame

Gen 4 · est. 17 m · Photographed twice by a League coastal patrol at Ras Hafun, a week apart, months after the Kafila ambush. In the first photograph its left thigh is torn open to the frame. In the second the wound has closed under a ridged, pinkish sheath.

- **What the evidence shows:** The Rakshasa's pattern grown up: the same welded Garrison-and-Tieniu skeleton, longer in the limbs and more upright, with fibre bundles that are now thicker, smoother and dry (no gel lines). On the left thigh, where the first photograph showed a tear, there is a ridged, pinkish, healed-over sheath, the only part of it that looks new.
- **Head:** As the Rakshasa's, with the welded plate gone and the lens cluster replaced by a single cracked sensor.
- **Weapons:** None visible.
- **Colours:** Sun-bleached primer and salt-crusted rust. The fibre is a darker grey-red; the healed sheath is pale pink.
- **The two details the photo must catch:** the pink sheath on the left thigh, and the fibre bundles in the shoulder gap. Everything else is haze.

The frame kneels at the waterline beside a beached dhow-ferry that is loading for the crossing, half hidden behind the dhow's sail and rigging. The focus has caught the rigging in the foreground, sharp, while the frame behind it is soft. Horizon tilted about 12 degrees, sky blown out white, salt spray on the lens.
