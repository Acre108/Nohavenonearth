## Mamont — first priority

Selected [scene](../output/imagegen/14-ob-17-mamont-scene.png) and [technical sheet](../output/imagegen/14-ob-17-mamont-technical-profile.png).

**Verdict: retain the design, rebuild the right arm/cannon articulation and correct the technical presentation.** The low head, forward hunch, massive shoulders, descending tusk-like chest plates and pillar legs convey the 112-tonne breakthrough role well. The main cannon is on its own right and the rotary is on its own left, as specified, but correct weapon side does not establish a complete arm. The industrial barrier-breaking scene works.

**Primary structural failure — right arm and cannon mount:** on reinspection, the sheet does not show a separately readable right upper arm, elbow and forearm beneath or behind the cannon. The shoulder, drum and gun assembly merge into a horizontal mass that reads as an arm replacement. Its aiming joints and recoil path are also unresolved, making the cannon look locked straight forward. The earlier statement that both arms were present was too confident and is withdrawn. Rebuild a clearly articulated right arm supporting the cannon, with visible shoulder and elbow joints, clearance from the chest tusks, and an explicit aiming/recoil mechanism. Include neutral and elevated/depressed gun poses plus a side view to demonstrate movement. Retain the separate left rotary arm. This is a design-readability failure; hidden geometry cannot be established from this single view.

1. **Scale reference fails.** The sheet’s human is roughly a quarter of the visible feet-to-head chassis height. A 1.8 m person against a 20.1 m frame should be about **8.96%** of standing chassis height. The current reference makes it read as a much smaller machine. Shrink the reference to roughly a third of its present height, then set it accurately against a neutral standing view. Preserve the heavy chassis proportions. A hunched pose and tall backpack are not suitable substitutes for a defined standing-height datum.
2. **Main bore reads far larger than 152 mm.** Perspective prevents an exact calibre measurement, but the dark muzzle opening is disproportionately large against the chassis. A 152 mm bore is only 0.76% of a 20.1 m chassis height before projection. Retain a heavy barrel jacket, but distinguish that jacket from the much smaller bore. Use a dimensioned orthographic inset.
3. **Rotary count needs correction/verification.** The muzzle has an excess-opening appearance; a clean six-barrel GSh-6M layout is not established. Require exactly six open barrel mouths in one ring around a solid central hub, with a clear muzzle inset, in both assets.
4. **Four-round autoloader is not demonstrated.** A drum is visible; its four-round capacity and feed into the right cannon are not explained. This is missing evidence, not proof that the drum holds the wrong number. Add a drum/feed detail.
5. **Requested turnaround is missing.** The sheet is a single three-quarter pose with a detached gun study. Add front, side and rear views showing the twin-diesel installation, cannon/drum support, and Grad-M mounting behind the drum.

Scene infantry and vehicles sit at different depths, so their screen heights do not establish an exact chassis height. Keep the dramatic composition; check same-plane scale cues during any regeneration. Do not propagate this sheet’s oversized human into related heavy-frame references.

**Suggested correction order:** resolve the right arm and cannon articulation first; rebuild the technical sheet with correct scale and equipment arrangement, then use that corrected sheet to repair the scene’s weapon details.

## Set-wide findings

- **Scale references:** the early selected sheets (01–22) consistently draw the person too large. These are visual estimates, not surveyed pixel measurements. Most are roughly 15–40% of the visible chassis height; the intended relationship is generally about 9–11%, or 13.8% for the 13 m Riggers. Use 1.8 m / specified standing height, with a shared floor baseline. The later Caribou, Wolverine, Medved-N, Mistral and Ghibli sheets are much closer.
- **Turnaround coverage:** most early sheets are three-quarter concept poses plus a weapon inset. They are useful design art, but do not satisfy front/side/rear or transformation requests. Tiebi and Rigger Security provide some additional views; the later five sheets provide much better design coverage.
- **Countable equipment:** Teamster/Bulwark pods, Medved rocket banks and the Russian-family rotary muzzles need explicit counted layouts. A solid central rotor hub must not become another open barrel.
- **Continuity:** weapon side, attachment and secondary frame identity sometimes change between scene and sheet. “Kestrel” has repeatedly been interpreted as a tiltrotor aircraft.
- Hidden components and tiny unreadable stencils are marked for clarification where relevant; they are not automatically failed as missing hardware.
